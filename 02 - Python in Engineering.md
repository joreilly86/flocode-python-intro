# Python In Engineering

## What is Python?

Python is a high-level, interpreted programming language that was first released in 1991. It was created by Guido van Rossum and named after the British comedy series "Monty Python's Flying Circus," starring John Cleese, my dad is a massive fan of his!

Soon after release Python gained popularity in academia and research circles, and later expanded to various industries, including web development, data science, artificial intelligence (AI) and the STEM and finance sectors.

Python's emphasis on simple syntax and readability really helps to reduce the cognitive overhead of learning the language, enabling a sharper focus on actually solving problems and finding solutions.

We’ll talk a little bit more about languages and syntax in the next module. For now lets focus on the benefits of Python for Engineers.

## Benefits of Python for Engineers

Python offers several key benefits that make it an attractive choice for engineers:

1. **Simplicity and Readability**: Python's clean and straightforward syntax makes it easy to read, write, and maintain code, reducing development time and increasing productivity.
2. **Vast, Specialized Libraries**: Python offers a rich ecosystem of engineering-focused libraries (such as NumPy, SciPy, among many more), streamlining analysis, computation, and visualization, providing ready-made modules and functions for engineering workflows. There are currently over 165,000 Python libraries available. Of course I only use a tiny fraction of these but the point is that the community is enormous and growing rapidly.
3. **Open-Source and Flexibility**: Python's open-source nature and flexibility empower engineers to create transparent custom tools, collaborate, and iterate on existing work. The beauty of open source development is that there are no black boxes, no secrets. If you think you can improve upon a project, then go ahead and do it! The possibilities for collaboration are huge.
4. **Modern Data Science and AI**: Python is the language of choice for modern data science and AI. As the engineering industry adapts to leverage more data-driven decision-making, the ability to interpret and manage data is becoming a crucial skill for engineers. We are already experiencing major adaptations in how we work as we use tools like ChatGPT and Llama to help facilitate administrative and creative tasks. This is a fun rabbit hole to enter, and we will be spending lots of time in here throughout Flocode’s future content.
5. **Overcoming Software Constraints**: Commercial engineering software, while extremely powerful, often comes with constraints such as costly licenses, rigid design, vendor lock-in, and a lack of transparency in how the software interprets certain situations or conditions. Python opens an entirely new dimension in terms of fine-tuned control and transparency, transforming the approach to problem-solving.

By leveraging Python, engineers can streamline their workflows, automate repetitive tasks, perform complex computations, analyze data, and integrate with other engineering software, ultimately enhancing their ability to tackle complex challenges more efficiently.

# Python Applications in Engineering

Python has numerous applications in the engineering field, including:

- **Scientific Computing:** Performing complex calculations, data modeling, and numerical simulations.
- **Data Analysis:** Extracting insights from large datasets, creating visualizations, and developing predictive models.
- **Automation:** Streamlining workflows, automating repetitive tasks, and integrating with engineering software.
- **Machine Learning:** Developing algorithms capable of learning from data to solve complex engineering challenges.
- **Libraries and Packages:** Python's extensive standard library provides a rich set of modules and tools, covering domains from geotechnical engineering, hydraulics, vibration analysis, complex finite element modeling, and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) to name just a few that are directly transferable to technical tasks, not to mention the opportunities for management, automation and custom tool development.

While each of these topics represents a vast domain with many sub-domains and specialties, the broader concepts of programming transcend any single language. They can be applied to achieve diverse objectives. Python stands out as the easiest language to learn, the most widely used in STEM fields, and as I mentioned has an incredibly rich ecosystem of libraries with a growing user base of learners around the world.

## Broader Impact

Python's impact extends far beyond these specific areas. Its versatility makes it a future-proof choice as we get inundated with new tools for this and for that. The rise of data science and AI has already begun reshaping our industry, and Python gives you a strong foothold in these areas, giving you the flexibility to adapt to the coming shifts. Unless you plan to ignore these tools, the benefits of learning the language are pretty obvious.

Learning Python cultivates a mindset of ‘Programmatic Thinking’, you can read all about that [here](https://open.substack.com/pub/flocode/p/027-programmatic-thinking-for-engineers?r=tbs50&utm_campaign=post&utm_medium=web) – breaking down complex problems into manageable components and approaching solutions logically. This way of thinking transcends coding itself, shaping how we approach problem-solving across all aspects of our work.

In our industry, adaptability is crucial, it always has been. Python equips us with the tools to future-proof our skillsets, ensuring relevance in an increasingly data-driven world. That’s not to say we abandon the fundamentals. 

My main message here is:

> **The tools may change but the principles don't.**

## Overcoming Misconceptions

There is a common misconception that Python is only suitable for technical specialists, developers working on structural software, or those involved in academic research. However, this greatly underestimates Python's practical use for engineers. With their strong foundation in logic and problem-solving, engineers are ideally suited to harness the power of Python. By dedicating a few weeks to focused learning, engineers can dramatically boost their workflow efficiency.

It's important to emphasize that mastering Python does require effort. While the rewards are enormous, the learning process may not always be easy. However, for dedicated engineers seeking to expand their capabilities, push beyond conventional limits, and solve problems on their own terms, there is no better path.

## Python's Role in AI and Machine Learning in Engineering

Let’s talk briefly about AI and the role of Machine Learning in Engineering. This is the elephant in the room in almost all knowledge based jobs. 

This is an incredible time for technology enthusiasts, AI is progressing in many domains at a blistering pace. But we do need to keep our feet on the ground. We need to think about the practical applications and the real world use cases. How and why should our clients pay for this work?

In the coming decade, the integration of Python and machine learning holds the potential to significantly enhance traditional civil and structural engineering practices. Deep learning capabilities are already surpassing previous analytical methods of analysis in certain sectors.

From optimizing structures to managing complex infrastructure, advanced applications are no longer solely in the hands of the tech industry – they're achievable by engineers who familiarize themselves with Python. Libraries like [TensorFlow](https://www.tensorflow.org/), [Keras](https://keras.io/), and [scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/) act as gateways to ML, making complex algorithms accessible without the need for a computer science PhD.

Our professional landscape is changing. There are already a a variety of available tools for you to take advantage of. Check out the podcast episodes I did with [Tim Rawling of CalcTree](https://open.substack.com/pub/flocode/p/025-flocode-podcast-005-tim-rawling?r=tbs50&utm_campaign=post&utm_medium=web) and Anande Bergman of Viktor AI to dig a little deeper into some options. These tools are actively taking shape, and evolving with their communities, providing opportunities for engineers with Python skills to progress and magnify their professional impact. Check it out at flocode.dev/about.

As the industry adapts, I see a few key areas for AI integration. Bear in mind we need standardized, high-fidelity datasets, which currently require a vast collaborative effort. 

- **Design Exploration**: Physics based AI can explore and optimize design parameters for strength, cost-efficiency, and sustainability, surpassing traditional methods. See Altair’s PhysicsAI and Ansys AI for more info. This is an exciting field that is quickly expanding. There’s a fantastic series on this topic by Steve Brunton ([Physics Informed Machine Learning](https://youtu.be/JoFW2uSd3Uo?si=OpX-RPvjTRAYhxr6)) that I highly recommend.
    
    [Physics Informed Machine Learning: High Level Overview of AI and ML in Science and Engineering](https://youtu.be/JoFW2uSd3Uo?si=OpX-RPvjTRAYhxr6)
    
- **Predictive Analytics**: Using Python's ML libraries for analyzing data from structures to predict deterioration, supporting proactive maintenance and extending infrastructure life. This extends to inspection work and drone operation, data collection and computer vision models that can automatically identify structural deficiencies as long as we train the models adequately.
- **Materials Innovation**: ML in Python has the potential to revolutionize materials innovation. Algorithms could  predict material properties, accelerate the discovery of new materials, and uncover hidden patterns in materials data. Success hinges on quality data collection and organization, but the possibilities for stronger, more sustainable, and even entirely new materials are immense.

While engineers are learning these new skills, we are crucial to curating datasets, interpreting results, and guiding what these algorithms should even try to predict.These capabilities are now within your reach. Through Python, you have the tools to think bigger and push the boundaries of what's possible in engineering.
